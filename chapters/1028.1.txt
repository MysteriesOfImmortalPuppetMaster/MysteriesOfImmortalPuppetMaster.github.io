23.09.2026

Getting beaten up by life...

Dear readers and friends who have been waiting all this time:
Hello everyone.

During this month-long hiatus, many of you left messages of concern, while quite a few others urged for updates. First of all, I want to solemnly say to you all: I'm sorry!

It is my fault for leaving everyone who cares about this story in suspense and waiting in vain for so long, and I have kept this debt of gratitude and guilt in my heart all along.

Let me honestly tell you all about my state during this period.

The first thing to knock on my door was physical illness.

It wasn't anything acute, but the accumulation of long hours at the desk and an irregular daily routine resulted in physical weakness, fatigue, and mental sluggishness. Just a little bit of prolonged brain work would make my mind feel tight and heavy. This was immediately followed by stubborn insomnia: lying awake for hours at night, yet feeling groggy and weak during the day. My whole body felt as if it were wrapped in shackles, unable to summon the energy to sit at the desk.

More difficult to endure than the physical ailment was the illness of the mind.

An evaluation at the hospital showed mild depression accompanied by an anxiety state. When I was in a good state, my mind was clear and I felt everything was manageable. Once an episode hit, I would fall into a state of powerlessness and self-doubt. Even though I knew I should be writing, I lacked the strength to put pen to paper. My emotions fluctuated wildly, like bobbing up and down in the waves, completely out of my own control.

In the early stage, I fell ill; in the later stage, it wasn't that I didn't want to write, but that I could no longer control my own mind and spirit.

I didn't stubbornly push through to the bitter end, nor did I run away from it.

Following the doctor's orders, I underwent medical intervention, cooperating with medication, adjustments to my routine, and psychological counseling. I also deliberately reduced information overload, forced myself to go outside for walks, and cleared my mind. This isn't something where a sudden breakthrough happens overnight; rather, it is moving forward bit by bit. Now, the insomnia has eased somewhat, the amplitude of my emotional roller coaster has narrowed, and the "fog" inside my brain is slowly clearing up.

According to the doctor's feedback, the overall treatment is progressing well, and I am also learning to coexist with this fluctuation-prone state rather than blindly fighting against it.

I also want to make my attitude clear here:

I will not use health issues as an eternal shield, nor am I playing the pity card to win sympathy. A broken update is a broken update, and the responsibility is mine. However, I don't want to force toothpaste out of a tube just to rush chapters when my mind and spirit are unstable - what comes out of that would not satisfy even myself, and it would fail the expectations everyone has held for this story and these words all these years.

Writing is my obsession, and it is also my responsibility. But the premise is that I must first stabilize myself.

As for the outlook ahead:

I cannot immediately resume updates right now; I always tend to overestimate myself and be overly optimistic. Next, I will first establish a steady writing rhythm, starting with short-period, fixed-duration desk sessions to gradually find my groove again. Once my condition is further consolidated, I will gradually bring the update frequency up to a sustainable level.

The framework of the story and the destinations of the characters - I have not let go of any of it. The foreshadowing I've buried and the plots that have lingered in my mind for a long time are all still there. It's just that I have learned one thing now: a good story cannot be rushed. And as a writer, one must first learn to treat oneself kindly in order to accompany the written word for a long time.

Thank you once again for everyone's patience, tolerance, and kindness; I also thank those of you who left messages reminding me to take care of my health. Thank you for once again illuminating me with starlight.

I will recuperate properly, write earnestly, and live up to this long-standing bond.

Gu Zhen Ren
